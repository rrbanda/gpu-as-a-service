Setting the Stage

Why GPUs — and Why “as a Service”?

What a GPU does

A CPU is a brilliant specialist — it solves complex problems one step at a time.
A GPU has thousands of small cores that do simple math in parallel — exactly what AI requires. Training runs billions of operations simultaneously. Inference multiplies millions of parameters per request. No other hardware can do this at AI speed.

⚡

The Power Grid Problem

Your organization bought 256 GPUs — the equivalent of building a private power plant. But instead of metering electricity by the kilowatt-hour, you gave each team a dedicated generator. Some run at 5% load while others have a waiting list. An unused GPU-hour, like an unsold airline seat, is gone forever.

🔓

The Missing Layer

Cloud providers solved this for CPU/memory years ago — you don’t buy a server, you request capacity and the platform finds it. For GPUs, that abstraction layer doesn’t exist yet. Most enterprises still manage GPUs like physical equipment, not like a utility.

💡

GPU as a Service

GPUaaS adds the utility layer. Teams request capacity (“I need 20 GB of GPU memory for 3 hours”), the platform finds the best fit, meters the usage, and reclaims capacity when the workload finishes. Your private power plant becomes a managed grid.

One sentence: GPU as a Service turns a fixed hardware investment into a metered utility — the right GPU, to the right workload, for the right duration, with cost visibility.
